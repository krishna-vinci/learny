      ## Abstract
      <!-- anchor: abstract -->

      This article explores the impact of the police action and the anti-communist struggle in Hyderabad on the formation of the Indian state in the first years after independence. Because of its central location and diverse cultural heritage, the absorption of the princely state of Hyderabad into the Indian Union was an important goal for Nehru's government. However, the task of bringing Hyderabad into the Union was not an easy one. As it entered Hyderabad, the government of independent India had to come to terms with the limitations of the police, military and bureaucracy, which it had inherited from the colonial state; and as it took over the governance of the state, it had to find ways to manage relations between Hindus and Muslims, even as the social order was being transformed. It also had to fight communism in the Telangana region of the state, whilst trying to ensure the loyalty of its new citizens. This article examines the ways in which India's first government confronted these complex problems. The following pages argue that these early years must be seen as a time of great dynamism, rather than as a period of stability inherited from the colonial state.

      ### Asaf Jah VII
      <!-- anchor: asaf-jah-vii -->

      [![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/Hyderabad_City_IB1142.jpg/250px-Hyderabad_City_IB1142.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](./File:Hyderabad_City_IB1142.jpg)

      Students leaving the [Osmania University](./Osmania_University "Osmania University"), c. 1939–1945

      Students leaving the Osmania University, c. 1939–1945

      The seventh and last [Nizam of Hyderabad](./Nizam_of_Hyderabad "Nizam of Hyderabad"), [Mir Osman Ali Khan](./Mir_Osman_Ali_Khan "Mir Osman Ali Khan") ascended the throne in 1911.[\[71\]](./History_of_Hyderabad#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaw,_Modern_Hyderabad_\(Deccan\)191428-71)

      He founded numerous institutions and public buildings in the city, including the [Begumpet Airport](./Begumpet_Airport "Begumpet Airport"), [Hyderabad State Bank](./Hyderabad_State_Bank "Hyderabad State Bank"), [Osmania University](./Osmania_University "Osmania University"), [Nizamia Observatory](./Nizamia_Observatory "Nizamia Observatory"), [Moazzam Jahi Market](./Moazzam_Jahi_Market "Moazzam Jahi Market"),[\[72\]](./History_of_Hyderabad#cite_note-72)[\[73\]](./History_of_Hyderabad#cite_note-73) [Nizamia Hospital](./Government_Nizamia_General_Hospital "Government Nizamia General Hospital"), [Assembly Hall](./Telangana_Legislature "Telangana Legislature") (formerly known as the Town Hall) [State Central Library](./State_Central_Library,_Hyderabad "State Central Library, Hyderabad") (formerly known as the *Asafiya Library*), [Hyderabad High Court](./High_Court_of_Judicature_at_Hyderabad "High Court of Judicature at Hyderabad").[\[74\]](./History_of_Hyderabad#cite_note-74) and the [Osmania General Hospital](./Osmania_General_Hospital "Osmania General Hospital"). The Nizam also invited British architect [Vincent Esch](./Vincent_Esch "Vincent Esch") for designing some of these. The Nizam also owned an airline, called the [Deccan Airways](./Deccan_Airways_Limited "Deccan Airways Limited").[\[75\]](./History_of_Hyderabad#cite_note-75)[\[76\]](./History_of_Hyderabad#cite_note-76)

      Two large reservoirs, namely the [Osman Sagar](./Osman_Sagar "Osman Sagar") and the [Himayat Sagar](./Himayat_Sagar "Himayat Sagar") were constructed a few kilometers west of Hyderabad to prevent another flood in the city, on the advice of [Sir M. Visvesvaraya](./M._Visvesvaraya "M. Visvesvaraya").[\[77\]](./History_of_Hyderabad#cite_note-77)[\[78\]](./History_of_Hyderabad#cite_note-78)

      He was known for his immense wealth, and [large jewel collection](./Nizam_jewels "Nizam jewels"), composed of 173 jewels, including the [Jacob Diamond](./Jacob_Diamond "Jacob Diamond").[\[79\]](./History_of_Hyderabad#cite_note-79) He was proclaimed the richest man in the world in 1937, and appeared on the cover of *[Time](./Time_\(magazine\) "Time (magazine)")* magazine owing majorly to the Golconda mines, which were the primary source of his wealth.
