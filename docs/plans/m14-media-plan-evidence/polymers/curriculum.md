# Curriculum

- [x] 01 — From familiar materials to atoms and molecules
  Scope: Elements, atoms, molecules, compounds and mixtures; read simple formulas using water, carbon and a plastic object as examples.
  Prerequisites: none
  Visual: diagram — Zoom from a familiar object to molecules and atoms, with scale labels
  Visual: chart — Element, compound and mixture shown as particle pictures
  Video: Khan Academy, Elements and atoms — an educator draws the basic building blocks; candidate awaiting verification
- [x] 02 — Bonds inside molecules, attractions between them
  Scope: Valence and simple covalent drawings for C, H, O and N; distinguish chemical bonds from intermolecular attractions and introduce polarity and hydrogen bonding qualitatively.
  Prerequisites: 01
  Visual: diagram — Methane and water with shared electrons and bond lines
  Visual: sketch/story — Strong connections along a chain versus attractions between chains
  Video: Crash Course Chemistry #22, Atomic Hook-Ups — visualize covalent sharing and unequal sharing; candidate awaiting verification
- [ ] 03 — Reactions and counting molecules
  Scope: Conservation of atoms, coefficients versus subscripts, balancing simple equations, molar mass and grams-to-moles conversions through worked examples.
  Prerequisites: 01, 02
  Visual: diagram — Atom inventory before and after a balanced reaction
  Visual: chart — Grams, moles and particles connected by labeled conversion factors
  Video: Crash Course Chemistry #6, Stoichiometry — worked mass-to-amount reasoning; candidate awaiting verification
- [ ] 04 — Carbon's building blocks
  Scope: Carbon chains, single and double bonds, simple structural and line drawings, and recognition of alcohol, carboxylic acid, amine, ester and amide groups without exhaustive naming rules.
  Prerequisites: 02, 03
  Visual: diagram — The same small molecule in expanded, condensed and line notation
  Visual: chart — Color-coded functional groups that recur in polymer chemistry
  Video: Follow a functional group from a monomer drawing to a linkage; no suitable video verified for this combined beginner need
- [ ] 05 — Monomers, repeat units and long chains
  Scope: Natural versus synthetic polymers, polymer versus plastic, monomer versus repeat unit, bracket notation, degree of polymerization and approximate chain mass; recognize that samples contain different chain lengths.
  Prerequisites: 03, 04
  Visual: diagram — Ethene, a polyethylene repeat unit and a chain with identifiable end groups
  Visual: chart — Toy chain-length distribution rather than one identical length for every molecule
  Video: TED-Ed, From DNA to Silly Putty by Jan Mattingly — animate small units building familiar natural and synthetic polymers; candidate awaiting verification
- [ ] 06 — Two ways to build polymers
  Scope: Worked polyethylene addition and polyester/nylon condensation examples; infer simple repeat units, track atoms and byproducts, and distinguish growth pattern from whether small molecules are lost without equating all step growth with condensation.
  Prerequisites: 04, 05
  Visual: diagram — A growing polyethylene chain alongside joining bifunctional polyester fragments
  Visual: diagram — Color-coded atom accounting for a condensation linkage and released water
  Video: Crash Course Chemistry #45, Polymers — illustrate addition and condensation examples with pause-and-draw practice; candidate awaiting verification
- [ ] 07 — Why a bag, bottle and rubber band behave differently
  Scope: Linear, branched and cross-linked chains; packing, amorphous and crystalline regions; thermoplastics, thermosets and elastomers; qualitative glass transition versus melting and material-property tradeoffs.
  Prerequisites: 02, 05, 06
  Visual: diagram — Chain packing and cross-links compared across polyethylene, PET and an elastomer
  Visual: chart — Qualitative stiffness versus temperature with glass transition and possible melting distinguished
  Video: Crash Course Engineering #20, The Polymer Explosion — connect chain motion and cross-linking to material behavior; candidate awaiting verification, thermal-transition coverage still missing
- [ ] 08 — Lifetime, recycling and a material-choice capstone
  Scope: Degradation versus fragmentation, hydrolysis of susceptible linkages, recycling and sorting limits, bio-based versus biodegradable materials, and evidence-based comparison of three everyday polymer products.
  Prerequisites: 03, 06, 07
  Visual: diagram — Product life cycle with reuse, mechanical recycling, chemical recycling and disposal routes
  Visual: chart — Bag, bottle and rubber band compared by structure, useful properties and end-of-life constraints
  Video: A beginner sorting-and-reprocessing demonstration showing contamination limits; no suitable free individual video verified
