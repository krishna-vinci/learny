      ## Standardized nomenclature
      <!-- anchor: standardized-nomenclature -->

      There are multiple conventions for naming polymer substances. Many commonly used polymers, such as those found in consumer products, are referred to by a common or trivial name. The trivial name is assigned based on historical precedent or popular usage rather than a standardized naming convention. Both the [American Chemical Society](./American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society") (ACS)[\[58\]](./Polymer#cite_note-58) and [IUPAC](./IUPAC "IUPAC")[\[59\]](./Polymer#cite_note-59) have proposed standardized naming conventions; the ACS and IUPAC conventions are similar but not identical.[\[60\]](./Polymer#cite_note-60) Examples of the differences between the various naming conventions are given in the table below:

      | Common name | ACS name | IUPAC name |
      | --- | --- | --- |
      | [Poly(ethylene oxide)](./Poly\(ethylene_oxide\) "Poly(ethylene oxide)") or PEO | Poly(oxyethylene) | Poly(oxyethylene) |
      | [Poly(ethylene terephthalate)](./Poly\(ethylene_terephthalate\) "Poly(ethylene terephthalate)") or PET | Poly(oxy-1,2-ethanediyloxycarbonyl-1,4-phenylenecarbonyl) | Poly(oxyethyleneoxyterephthaloyl) |
      | [Nylon 6](./Nylon_6 "Nylon 6") or Polyamide 6 | Poly\[imino(1-oxo-1,6-hexanediyl)\] | Poly\[azanediyl(1-oxohexane-1,6-diyl)\] |

      In both standardized conventions, the polymers' names are intended to reflect the monomer(s) from which they are synthesized (source based nomenclature) rather than the precise nature of the repeating subunit. For example, the polymer synthesized from the simple alkene [ethene](./Ethene "Ethene") is called polyethene, retaining the *\-ene* suffix even though the double bond is removed during the polymerization process:

      [![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/27/Ethene_polymerization.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](./File:Ethene_polymerization.png)→[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Polyethylene_repeat_unit.svg/120px-Polyethylene_repeat_unit.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](./File:Polyethylene_repeat_unit.svg)

      However, IUPAC structure based nomenclature is based on naming of the preferred [constitutional repeating unit](./Repeat_unit "Repeat unit").[\[61\]](./Polymer#cite_note-61)

      IUPAC has also issued guidelines for abbreviating new polymer names.[\[62\]](./Polymer#cite_note-62) 138 common polymer abbreviations are also standardized in the standard [ISO](./ISO "ISO") 1043–1.[\[63\]](./Polymer#cite_note-63)

      ### Monomers and repeat units
      <!-- anchor: monomers-and-repeat-units -->

      The identity of the repeat units (monomer residues, also known as "mers") comprising a polymer is its first and most important attribute. Polymer nomenclature is generally based upon the type of monomer residues comprising the polymer. A polymer which contains only a single type of [repeat unit](./Repeat_unit "Repeat unit") is known as a **homopolymer**, while a polymer containing two or more types of repeat units is known as a **[copolymer](./Copolymer "Copolymer")**.[\[22\]](./Polymer#cite_note-22) A **terpolymer** is a copolymer which contains three types of repeat units.[\[23\]](./Polymer#cite_note-23)

      [Polystyrene](./Polystyrene "Polystyrene") is composed only of [styrene](./Styrene "Styrene")\-based repeat units, and is classified as a homopolymer. [Polyethylene terephthalate](./Polyethylene_terephthalate "Polyethylene terephthalate"), even though produced from two different [monomers](./Monomer "Monomer") ([ethylene glycol](./Ethylene_glycol "Ethylene glycol") and [terephthalic acid](./Terephthalic_acid "Terephthalic acid")), is usually regarded as a homopolymer because only one type of repeat unit is formed. [Ethylene-vinyl acetate](./Ethylene-vinyl_acetate "Ethylene-vinyl acetate") contains more than one variety of repeat unit and is a copolymer. Some biological polymers are composed of a variety of different but structurally related monomer residues; for example, [polynucleotides](./Polynucleotide "Polynucleotide") such as DNA are composed of four types of [nucleotide](./Nucleotide "Nucleotide") subunits.

      ### Morphology
      <!-- anchor: morphology -->

      Polymer morphology generally describes the arrangement and microscale ordering of polymer chains in space. The macroscopic physical properties of a polymer are related to the interactions between the polymer chains.

      -   **Disordered polymers:** In the solid state, atactic polymers, polymers with a high degree of [branching](./Branching_\(polymer_chemistry\) "Branching (polymer chemistry)") and random copolymers form [amorphous](./Amorphous_solid "Amorphous solid") (i.e. glassy structures).[\[45\]](./Polymer#cite_note-MakroChem-45) In melt and solution, polymers tend to form a constantly changing "statistical cluster", see [freely-jointed-chain model](./Freely_Jointed_Chain "Freely Jointed Chain"). In the [solid state](./Solid "Solid"), the respective [conformations](./Protein_conformation "Protein conformation") of the molecules are frozen. Hooking and entanglement of chain molecules lead to a "mechanical bond" between the chains. [Intermolecular](./Intermolecular_force "Intermolecular force") and intramolecular attractive forces only occur at sites where molecule segments are close enough to each other. The irregular structures of the molecules prevent a narrower arrangement.

      -   **Linear polymers** with periodic structure, low branching and stereoregularity (e. g. not atactic) have a [semi-crystalline](./Semi-crystalline "Semi-crystalline") structure in the solid state.[\[45\]](./Polymer#cite_note-MakroChem-45) In simple polymers (such as polyethylene), the chains are present in the crystal in zigzag conformation. Several zigzag conformations form dense chain packs, called crystallites or lamellae. The lamellae are much thinner than the polymers are long (often about 10 nm).[\[46\]](./Polymer#cite_note-KunstChemIng-46) They are formed by more or less regular folding of one or more molecular chains. Amorphous structures exist between the lamellae. Individual molecules can lead to entanglements between the lamellae and can also be involved in the formation of two (or more) lamellae (chains than called tie molecules). Several lamellae form a superstructure, a [spherulite](./Spherulite_\(polymer_physics\) "Spherulite (polymer physics)"), often with a diameter in the range of 0.05 to 1 mm.[\[46\]](./Polymer#cite_note-KunstChemIng-46)

      The type and arrangement of (functional) residues of the repeat units effects or determines the crystallinity and strength of the secondary valence bonds. In isotactic polypropylene, the molecules form a helix. Like the zigzag conformation, such helices allow a dense chain packing. Particularly strong intermolecular interactions occur when the residues of the repeating units allow the formation of [hydrogen bonds](./Hydrogen_bond "Hydrogen bond"), as in the case of [*p*\-aramid](./Aramid "Aramid"). The formation of strong intramolecular associations may produce diverse folded states of single linear chains with distinct [circuit topology](./Circuit_topology "Circuit topology"). Crystallinity and superstructure are always dependent on the conditions of their formation, see also: [crystallization of polymers](./Crystallization_of_polymers "Crystallization of polymers"). Compared to amorphous structures, semi-crystalline structures lead to a higher stiffness, density, melting temperature and higher resistance of a polymer.

      -   **Cross-linked polymers:** Wide-meshed cross-linked polymers are [elastomers](./Elastomer "Elastomer") and cannot be molten (unlike [thermoplastics](./Thermoplastic "Thermoplastic")); heating cross-linked polymers only leads to [decomposition](./Thermal_decomposition "Thermal decomposition"). [Thermoplastic elastomers](./Thermoplastic_elastomer "Thermoplastic elastomer"), on the other hand, are reversibly "physically crosslinked" and can be molten. Block copolymers in which a hard segment of the polymer has a tendency to crystallize and a soft segment has an amorphous structure are one type of thermoplastic elastomers: the hard segments ensure wide-meshed, physical crosslinking.

      ### Functional Groups with Carbon–Carbon Multiple Bonds
