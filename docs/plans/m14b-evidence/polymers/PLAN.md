## Goal
Build confidence in chemistry from near-zero foundations, then explain how polymers are made and why their molecular structures produce useful materials. This is a first foundation toward mastery, not a promise of professional expertise in one course.

## Scope — in
Atoms and simple bonding; reading organic structures; functional groups; reactions and basic mole calculations; monomers and repeat units; chain-growth and step-growth polymerization; chain architecture and molecular-size distributions; interactions, crystallinity and thermal behavior; material selection, degradation and recycling. Examples include polyethylene, PET, nylon, rubber and cellulose.

## Scope — out
A complete general- or organic-chemistry course, quantum-mechanical derivations, advanced reaction kinetics, specialist catalysts, instrument operation, industrial reactor design and unsupervised chemical synthesis. Linear algebra and ML are not objectives for this study set.

## Pace and approach
Allow approximately 14–18 weeks at four hours per week, with no deadline. Most chapters take one week; bonding, polymerization and properties may take two. Start with an everyday object and a picture, then a worked example, then just enough terminology and theory.

A weekly rhythm: two 45-minute evening sessions for pictures and examples, one 90-minute weekend practice session, and one hour for retrieval, corrections and a small application. Python is optional for a chain-length histogram; paper sketches suffice. No paid or hosted-only tooling is required: use downloaded readings where permitted, paper or local Markdown, and optional local Python. Videos are optional supplements, not compulsory external services.

## Progress checks
Finish each chapter with an annotated sketch, three short explanation questions and one worked problem. Move on when you can explain the central idea without copying and solve a new example; otherwise revisit the example before increasing difficulty.

Milestones: after 04, interpret a simple structure and reaction; after 07, draw repeat units and distinguish growth patterns; after 10, explain structure–property links with qualifications. Finish with a one-page comparison of a bag, bottle and rubber band, connecting chemistry, structure, properties and end-of-life options. Assess explanations against the readings, not brand names or recycling symbols alone.

## Reading strategy
Use the chosen OpenStax introduction as a short organic-chemistry orientation, not as a full foundations course. Use Wikipedia selectively for examples and overview, avoiding its damaged extracted equations. Use Schaller for selected diagrams and exercises after the chemistry bridge; its registered extraction currently contains only the landing page. Additional chapter URLs need learner-approved ingestion before becoming assigned readings.

## Safety
Activities are drawing, calculations and observation of ordinary objects only. No burning, solvent tests, heating unknown plastics, nylon synthesis or handling reaction chemicals at home. Laboratory demonstrations are observation-only.
