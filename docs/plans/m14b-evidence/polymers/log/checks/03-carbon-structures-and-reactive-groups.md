## Issues

### 1. Minor — `R`/`R′` are defined too narrowly

- **Claim:** `R` and `R′` stand for “the remaining carbon-containing parts” of a structure (note §2, sentence after the carbonyl explanation; repeated in the visual caption).
- **Source:** Western Oregon University, `parsed/01-part.md#drawing-abbreviated-organic-structures` (lines 297–303) describes `R` as a way to designate “part of a molecule outside of the region of interest.” The same note’s functional-group patterns include cases where the simplest substituent can be hydrogen (for example, formic acid, H–C(=O)–OH).
- **Verdict:** Partially supported; the note correctly presents `R`/`R′` as placeholders for the rest of a structure, but the blanket “carbon-containing” wording is narrower than the general notation.
- **Problem:** It could lead a learner to think the patterns exclude valid simplest members such as formic acid or formamide.
- **Fix:** Say “`R` and `R′` stand for the rest of a structure; in these examples they usually represent carbon-containing parts.” Keep the neighboring explanation that they are placeholders and need not be identical.
