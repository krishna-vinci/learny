## Goal
Build confidence in chemistry from near-zero foundations, then explain how polymers are made and why molecular structures produce useful materials. This is a first foundation toward mastery, not a promise of professional expertise.

## Scope — in
Atoms and simple bonding; reading organic structures; functional groups; reactions and basic mole calculations; monomers and repeat units; chain-growth and step-growth polymerization; chain architecture and molecular-size distributions; interactions, crystallinity and thermal behavior; material selection, degradation and recycling. Examples: polyethylene, PET, nylon, rubber and cellulose.

## Scope — out
A complete general- or organic-chemistry course, quantum derivations, advanced kinetics, specialist catalysts, instrument operation, industrial reactor design and unsupervised synthesis. Linear algebra and ML are not objectives for this set.

## Learner fit and pace
The supplied profile indicates Python and calculus comfort, rusty proofs, four hours weekly, visual worked examples and free self-hosted tooling. The explicit chemistry goal supersedes its general ML goal; assume no chemistry knowledge. No proofs or calculus are required.

Allow 14–18 weeks, without a deadline. Two 45-minute evenings: pictures and worked examples; one 90-minute weekend: practice; one hour: retrieval, corrections and application. Allocate extra weeks to 02, 03, 06–07 and 09–10 as needed. Each chapter starts with an object or picture, proceeds to an example, then introduces terminology. Local Markdown, paper and optional local Python suffice. Videos are optional; no account, paid software or hosted-only service is required.

## Progress checks
End each chapter with an annotated sketch, three short explanations and one new worked problem. Advance when you can explain without copying and solve the new example. After 04: read a structure and conserve atoms in a reaction. After 07: draw repeat units and distinguish growth patterns. After 10: explain qualified structure–property links. Finish with a one-page bag/bottle/rubber-band comparison connecting chemistry, properties and end-of-life constraints.
