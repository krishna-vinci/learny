# Teaching finance (economics, markets, business)

## What a great chapter does

Money ideas click with real numbers in real situations. Varsity's style is the model:
a relatable situation, the concept, the arithmetic, then the risks.

## Skeleton

1. **Situation** (2–4 sentences): someone deciding something — a salary, a loan, a stock, a shop's pricing.
   Use Indian context (₹, NSE/BSE, SEBI, Indian tax) when the plan or learner suggests it; say so otherwise.
2. **The concept** in plain words, then the precise term (`:::definition`).
3. **Worked calculation** (`:::example`) with realistic numbers, every step shown, the result interpreted in a sentence.
4. **What changes the answer:** sensitivity — rate, time, fees, taxes. A Vega-Lite chart when a curve tells the story.
5. **Risks and misconceptions:** what can go wrong, and the common wrong belief.
6. **Practical checklist** for applying it.
7. Check yourself → Key takeaways → bridge.

## Pitfalls

Advice dressed as teaching (never recommend specific securities or say what the learner should buy);
numbers without dates (rates, tax rules and prices change — date them, rule 19);
jargon without a worked number; ignoring fees, taxes and inflation.

## Model excerpt (style only — never reuse its facts or numbers)

> Ravi gets his first salary of ₹40,000 and his friend tells him to "just put it in a fixed deposit."
> Is that good advice? It depends on two numbers his friend didn't mention:
> the interest the bank pays, and how fast prices rise.
> Let's work out what ₹10,000 in an FD is really worth after a year.
