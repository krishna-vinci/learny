# Teaching history (and humanities, social sciences)

## What a great chapter does

History is a story about people making choices under constraints. Tell it as one.
The learner should finish able to retell the story, explain *why* it happened,
and say how we know.

## Skeleton

1. **Scene** (2–4 sentences): drop the learner into a moment — a place, a year, a person, a decision.
2. **The question the period answers** in plain words ("How did a fort town become a capital?").
3. **Narrative sections**, in time order, each built around people and turning points.
   Name actors, give dates, and keep a clear thread: situation → choice → consequence.
4. **Causes and consequences:** a short section or table separating long-term causes, triggers and results.
5. **Perspectives:** where accounts differ (rulers vs subjects, colonial vs Indian, official vs memoir),
   say who claims what and why the difference matters. Never flatten a contested event into one voice.
6. **Timeline** (table or Vega-Lite chart) only for the dates the learner should keep.
7. **What you can still see today**, when relevant: monuments, street names, institutions, food, language.
8. Check yourself → Key takeaways → bridge.

## Blocks

`:::example` to read one source closely (a monument, an inscription, a treaty clause, a photograph).
`:::definition` for period terms (paramountcy, jagir, Residency). `:::deeper` for historiography.
No `:::theorem`.

## Pitfalls

Single-cause stories; anachronism (judging 1700 with 2020 vocabulary without saying so);
inventing dialogue or motives the sources don't support; date lists without a story;
treating today's scene as an unchanged survival.

## Model excerpt (style only — never reuse its facts or numbers)

> It is 1687, and the walls of Golconda have held out for eight months.
> Aurangzeb's army is camped below; inside, Abul Hasan Qutb Shah still holds court.
> The fort does not fall to cannon in the end — a gate is opened from within.[^src:…]
> To understand why a dynasty that built Charminar ended this way, we need to step back
> and look at the Deccan the Mughals had been eyeing for decades.
