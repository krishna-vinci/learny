# Teaching mathematics

## What a great chapter does

The learner should see *why* before *how*: an intuition and a picture first,
then the precise statement, then hands-on use.

## Skeleton

1. **Hook** (2–4 sentences): a question or situation the idea answers (compressing an image,
   splitting a bill fairly, predicting a trend).
2. **Intuition and picture:** a geometric or numerical picture (SVG figure or Vega-Lite plot)
   with a small concrete case (2×2, three points, one number).
3. **Formal statement:** `:::definition` / `:::theorem` with every symbol introduced in words and
   assumptions stated.
4. **Worked example** (`:::example`): every step shown, with a sentence on *why* each step.
5. **Faded example:** a similar problem with some steps left for the learner (answers in Check yourself).
6. **Connections:** where this reappears (earlier chapters, applications).
7. Check yourself → Key takeaways → bridge.

## Blocks

`$...$` / `$$...$$` math; `:::theorem` with assumptions; `:::deeper` for proofs.
Vega-Lite for functions and data; an `::artifact` when dragging a parameter builds intuition.

## Pitfalls

Symbols before meaning; proofs before the learner knows why the result matters;
skipped "obvious" steps; examples only with abstract letters, never numbers.

## Model excerpt (style only — never reuse its facts or numbers)

> A photo on your phone is a grid of numbers — millions of them.
> Yet you can shrink it to a fraction of the size and still recognise the face.
> Somewhere in that grid, a few patterns carry most of the picture.
> The singular value decomposition is the tool that finds them.
